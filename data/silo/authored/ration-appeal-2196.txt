Ration Appeals Panel, Case 2196

The appellant, a soil tender of the Farms on Level 70, appealed the reduction
of her household allowance following the death of her husband in year 216.

The reduction was applied automatically on the recording of the death, as the
schedule requires. The appellant's case is that her household did not shrink,
because her husband's mother has lived in the apartment since year 209 and is
recorded at her own former address, which has stood empty for eleven years and
which Supply has continued to allow against.

The panel finds for the appellant on the facts and against her on the remedy.
The household is three and was three. But the allowance that should be restored
to it is the one attached to the mother, not the one attached to the husband,
and that allowance has been drawn at the empty apartment for eleven years by
nobody the panel can name.

Supply is directed to correct the residence record, restore the appellant's
household to a three-person allowance from the date of this finding, and report
to the panel on how an allowance is drawn at an address where no person is
recorded as living.

The panel observes, without making it a finding, that it has heard four appeals
this year that turned on a residence record disagreeing with where people
actually sleep, and that in all four the record was the thing that was wrong.
The schedule assumes the record is right. The panel is no longer confident that
assumption is safe, and has written separately to the Warden of Records.
