Nursery Note on the Lottery Rolls, Year 219

The Nursery keeps two rolls. The first is of those entered in the lottery. The
second is of those who have won and not yet borne, which the Nursery calls the
open roll.

A name leaves the open roll in one of three ways: a birth, the end of the
allotted year, or a death. The Nursery records which. Over the ten years to 219
the open roll closed two thousand one hundred and forty times, of which one
thousand five hundred and ninety were births.

This note concerns the remainder, and one thing about it that the Nursery
cannot explain and does not wish to leave unrecorded.

Of the five hundred and fifty closures that were not births, the share ending
in the allotted year running out is even across the sections in every year but
one. In year 214 the Down Deep share was more than twice its usual. The Nursery
has checked the entries and found no error in them. Medical has checked its own
records for that year and reports nothing unusual.

Year 214 is the year of the Level 142 cistern failure, and Down Deep was on
carried water for eleven hours and on reduced ration for nine days after. The
Nursery observes the coincidence and makes no claim about it. Nine days is
short and the effect, if it is one, is large.

What the Nursery asks is that the rolls be kept by section from now on rather
than in one book, so that the next time a thing like this happens it can be seen
in the year it happens rather than five years later by someone reading back.
