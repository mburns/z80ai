Judicial Direction 220-1: On What the Archive May Be Asked

The archive terminal answers questions about persons recorded in it. This
direction concerns what may be done with those answers and is addressed to
every department that holds a terminal.

An answer from the archive is a record and not a finding. It states what was
entered. It does not state that what was entered is true, and no proceeding may
treat it as more than the entry it reports. The Ration Appeals Panel has now
twice found a residence record wrong, and a direction that assumed otherwise
would have made those findings impossible.

The archive answers about a person by name. Where two persons share a name the
archive resolves the question to one of them, and it does so without saying
that it has done so. It is the asker's duty to establish that the person the
archive answered about is the person they asked about. The Judicial has seen
one matter in which it was not, and the answer in that matter was correct in
every particular about a man who had been dead for nine years.

The archive does not answer what it was not asked. Where it has nothing it says
so, and where it has something adjacent it may offer that instead. An adjacent
answer is not an answer and is not to be recorded as one.

Finally: the archive is not a witness and may not be cited as one. It is a book
that has been asked to turn its own pages. The person who asked it, and who read
what it turned to, is the witness, and it is their reading that stands.
