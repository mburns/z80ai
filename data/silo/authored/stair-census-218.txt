On the Counting of the Stair, Year 218

A note by the Warden of Records, filed with the Nursery.

Twice in living memory the silo has counted itself by having every person stand
at their own door on the same morning. The first count, in year 174, returned a
number four hundred and six higher than the ledgers. The second, in year 218,
returned a number one hundred and eleven lower.

Neither result was published in the way the other was. The first was announced
in the Cafeteria and the ledgers corrected to match it. The second was reported
to the Judicial and has been under review since, which is a way of saying it has
not been reported at all.

This note is not an argument that either count was right. It is a record that
they were done differently. In 174 the counters walked down and the count began
at the top. In 218 the counters walked up, and the count began Down Deep at the
same hour that the Mids were still asleep. The Mids were counted last on both
occasions and the two counts disagree about the Mids by very nearly the whole
of their difference.

The Warden's view is that a count taken while people are moving counts the
stair and not the silo, and that a silo of a hundred and forty-four levels has
no hour at which nobody is on the stair. Any third count should stand every
counter still and move nobody, which means closing the stair for a morning, and
the Warden is aware that no committee has ever agreed to that.

Filed with the Nursery rather than the Judicial, because the Nursery keeps what
it is given and the Judicial keeps what it decides to keep.
