Incident Report 214-11: Cistern Pump Failure, Level 142

At approximately 0340 on the eleventh day of the two hundred and fourteenth
year, the primary cistern pump on Level 142 stopped without warning. The Third
Shift pump operator on duty logged the silence before the pressure alarm
reached her, which is the only reason the loss was held to eleven hours of
supply rather than the full reserve.

Water Treatment attended within the hour. The fault was traced to the lower
bearing housing, where a seal had been weeping for long enough to leave a salt
line the width of a thumb. The weep does not appear in any maintenance log for
the preceding two years. Mechanical has since confirmed that the housing is one
of four of its pattern still in service and that no spare exists below Level
120.

Supply was restored at 1450 the same day by cross-feeding from the secondary
cistern, a procedure last rehearsed in year 209 and not since. Two of the four
valve stations required force to turn. One is recorded here as seized and has
been referred to Mechanical as its own work order.

The panel's finding is that the failure was not sudden. A seal that leaves a
salt line has been failing for months, and nobody whose duty it was to look at
that housing was told that looking at it was their duty. The rota for Level 142
lists the pump and does not list the housing. Down Deep has run on that
distinction since the rota was written.

Recommendation: fold the bearing housings into the pump entry on every rota
below Level 120, and rehearse the cross-feed annually rather than when it is
needed.
