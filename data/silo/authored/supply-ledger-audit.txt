Supply: Audit of the Standing Ledgers, Year 216

The Quartermaster ordered a full audit of the standing ledgers, the first since
year 201, following the panel's direction in Case 2196.

Every allowance drawn in year 215 was matched against the residence record. Of
eleven thousand four hundred and six allowances, eleven thousand two hundred and
ninety matched. One hundred and sixteen did not.

Of the hundred and sixteen, ninety-four are people who moved and whose ledger
followed them while the residence record did not. Those are corrected. Fourteen
are people recorded at two addresses, all of which resolve to a marriage where
one partner's move was entered and the other's was not. Those are corrected.

Eight remain. In each of the eight an allowance is drawn at an address where the
residence record shows nobody, and in each the ledger entry was opened before
year 200 and has been continued by hand at every audit since without a name
against it. Two of the eight are on Level 3.

The Quartermaster has closed all eight and has written to the Sheriff's Office
rather than to the panel, and records here that he expects no reply and is
making the record for the sake of the record.

The audit further finds that the ledgers are kept in a hand that changes every
few years and never carries a signature, so there is no way to ask the person
who continued an entry why they continued it. From this year every ledger page
is signed. It is a small thing and it should have been done in 201.
